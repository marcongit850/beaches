Walton County's beaches are not simply public or private. Court rulings, a repealed state law, a 2017 ordinance, a large lawsuit, private settlements, and beach-restoration law left a patchwork. This site is the record of what still applies.

Questions

Can I walk the whole shoreline?

Visit South Walton says yes. You may traverse the wet sand for the entire 26 miles, from a lawful entry. See Wet sand on the Rules page. Their FAQ: Beach access FAQ.

Is the dry sand public?

No, not as a blanket rule. See the Rules page.

Is the dry sand private?

No, not as a blanket rule. Public beaches, state parks, and signed settlement beaches are different. See the Rules page.

Does every private beach have a 20-foot public strip?

No. Only owners who signed the 2023 settlement. See Signed 2023 settlement beaches and the settlement terms on the Timeline.

Did the 2026 appeal make every beach private?

No. It held that the February 2024 customary-use judgment no longer has legal effect. It did not decide the settlement, and it did not say customary use disappeared. See February 18, 2026 on the Timeline.

Can I walk through a beachfront yard to reach the water?

Not necessarily. The settlement says the public cannot enter a participating parcel to reach the 20-foot zone. See Signed 2023 settlement beaches and the settlement terms.

Visit South Walton tells visitors the current beach rules. This site explains the history, the votes, and the cases.
